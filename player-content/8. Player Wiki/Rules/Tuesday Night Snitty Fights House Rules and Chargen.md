## Agile Maneuvers & Weapon Finesse + Combat Maneuvers

As written, per a FAQ, you can only use dex on combat maneuvers via weapon finesse if you’re using a finesse weapon to do the maneuver and only a few maneuvers let you do that. You need agile maneuvers to apply your dex to combat maneuvers in general. This FAQ is dumb. *You can finesse an unarmed strike, FFS.* If you have weapon finesse you can use your dex for whatever combat maneuver you want. They’re hard enough to pull off without an extra feat tax for dex builds.

## Archery

Archery in PF1 is very good. (It was pretty bad in 3.x.) It’s not brokenly good where the game just craps itself and we all go home and cry, but it’s probably better than it needs to be. I don’t want to nerf it into oblivion, but would like it to slip back to more on parity with melee. I’m also cognizant that archery pays hefty feat taxes, which can be punitive to the point that you only ever want to start as an archer if you are a human. So two modest changes:

1. Precise shot no longer has prerequisites. You can just be an elf with one feat and take it at first level instead of being forced to be a human or eat -4 to your attacks for most of book 1.
2. Clustered shots no longer combines all damage from a full attack into one for purposes of DR. Instead it allows you to ignore 1 point of the foe’s DR, +1 point per four points of base attack bonus.

## Combat Expertise

This is a feat tax that no one takes if they can help it, to the point where even Paizo wrote workarounds. Fuck it. Anything that had Combat Expertise as a prerequisite no longer does.

## Coming Back from the Dead

Raise dead, reincarnate, resurrection, and true resurrection have no gp cost beyond the normal charge for NPCs casting a spell. However, they tend to be reserved preferentially for people of the caster’s faith and depending on circumstances there may be a waiting list. If the deceased is known to the caster as a worshiper in good standing, one may jump up the list, even to the front of the line and/or get a reduced rate. Faiths that will resurrect anyone exist, but they tend to charge more (50% or more above normal) and have longer waiting lists. These restrictions do not apply to PCs doing the spellcasting; there’s no religious prohibition on raising your friends and allies for free.

## Damage Reduction and Riders

Lots of monsters have DR. Rules as written, if the DR negates the full amount of damage that lands it also negates any rider effect you had on that damage, like stunning fist, injury-based diseases or poisons, etc. As a house rule, this also applies to precision damage as follows:

If the attack’s damage is fully negated prior to applying precision damage, the precision damage also doesn’t land. It’s just intuitively weird to me for the rules to simultaneously say that your sneak attack represents finding a vital spot and your weapon to deal zero damage but you get extra anyway. This is unlikely to come up very often, even for stuff like a rogue sticking stuff with knives since you’ll be using dex-to-damage and by the time DR10 and DR15 show up you can probably get around them in other ways. Should rogues and other precision-focused PCs end up too hard up, we’ll of course revise.

## Divine Spellcasting

This has been a setting assumption before, but I don’t think I ever spelled it out: If you are using divine magic, and are not an oracle, then anything you do that has a verbal component includes an invocation of your deity which is recognizable to people hearing you cast. You can disguise your affiliation by using titles or aliases, with silent spell, etc. This is 100% a flavor thing but you are allowed and encouraged to ask me who NPC divines are invoking when they start casting at you. Oracles can do this or not since the whole point of the class is that the exact origin of their powers is mysterious.

## Fractional Base Bonuses

If you are not planning to multiclass, none of this matters to you. If you are, then you’ve probably noticed that it’s easy to end up with your BAB or your saves a bit out of whack one way or another. For example, say a wizard multiclasses into another class with a good will save, then a prestige class with the same. The baseline will save just jacked up by four over all of…two levels. At the same time, the BAB might remain zero. This isn’t game-breaking and it’s not likely that someone in this group is going to unwittingly reach level 3 with a +0 BAB, but to smooth the curve a bit we’ll use the Fractional Base Bonuses variant from Unchained.

## Fly

When fly was dispelled or ran out of duration, you used to just hit the ground because of gravity. It does that again. Fly also fails this way if you end up carrying more than your maximum load. Feather fall is a first level spell you can cast as an immediate action and it’s also available as a ring. Most falls aren’t going to be lethal to PCs likely to be taking them anyway. I’ve found the parachute clause in fly a little silly ever since it was introduced to the game.

## Haste

3.0 haste got a bad rep for being stupidly OP...which it was really not. It was good, sure, but by the time it could be reliably leveraged to do lots of neat stuff you also had mass haste so the whole party got in on the fun. The nerf to it was also overcomplicated and someone misremembers it about half the time it comes up. A simpler solution:

1. Haste is a single target spell with a range of close.
2. Haste grants you a +1 bonus on attack rolls and a +1 dodge bonus to AC and Reflex saves. It does not grant an increase to your movement speed.
3. Haste grants you a partial action you may take before or after your regular turn. (If you just cast haste, it has to be after, obvs.) WTF is a partial action? It’s a term that 3.0 had and 3.5 retired…except the concept sort of stuck around but got explained worse, a tradition I continued. So here’s [the straight shit](https://www.dandwiki.com/wiki/3e_SRD:Partial_Actions).

The long and short of it is that a partial action is equivalent to anything you could do with a standard action, minus the move. It’s like 55% of your normal action. There’s a table at the link, but basically you can use a partial action to take a single attack (at best BAB), cast a spell with the normal 1-action casting time, OR use it to do a move or move action. This is in addition to anything else you get in the round. So you could use your partial to move and then full attack, or get an extra attack on a full attack when you’re in reach, or you could cast two 1-action spells, etc.

There is a party buff version of haste, Mass Haste. It’s seventh level and targets one creature per level, no two of which can be more than 30 ft. apart.

## Healing

1. Spells that are classified as conjuration (healing) in PF1 are hereby reclassified to necromancy (healing). This change is purely of flavor significance. Necromancy in the game has long been the magic of life and death, both of which are natural forces in the same way as fire and water are. This is to emphasize that.
2. Breath of Life can be cast spontaneously like a cure spell by clerics and company who channel positive energy and do spontaneous cures. For negative energy types, the equivalent ability is Slay Living. Breath of Life and Raise Dead both now work on [death] effects. You will need resurrection or true resurrection in certain circumstances, the most likely of which is a body being largely destroyed by something like disintegrate, stolen, or having your soul stolen.
3. When used out of combat, a Cure X Wounds spell/wand/whatever heals the average roll, rounded up. We do it already but it never got written down. So a wand of CLW heals 6 (4.5, rounded up to 5, +1), etc.

## Item Slots

It used to be that you could pretty much make a slotted item and put it wherever. The item slot listed was the most common and stuff you wanted more than one of, like stat buffs, tended to live in multiple slots so you could do that. For example, you might have a belt of giant strength +4 and gloves of dexterity +2 or whatever and they were priced accordingly instead of being up charged because you moved something out of a slot. Consolidating all those items to two slots did not improve the Big Six problem. It just meant you got less or paid a lot more.

1. The Belt and Headband slots are generally available as-is. You can get any of your mentals in a headband and physicals in a belt, right off the shelf. You can also get them off the shelf in their 3e-era slots. Str comes in both belts and gauntlets. Dex comes in gloves. You can get your Con in an amulet. Intelligence comes in headbands (no change, sorry). Wisdom is something you can also wear on your neck, usually in a periapt. Charisma also comes in cloaks.
2. However, you can order custom items or have someone in the party create them for you so that they fit in other slots. In fact, you can do that for any slotted item. Rather use your cloak slot for something other than saves? Take a ring that costs as much as the cloak would have. Or make lucky boots that do the same thing. The only time you’ll take an extra cost penalty on slotted items is by making them slotless or consolidating multiple effects into a single slot.

## Knowledge (arcana) and Spellcraft

These are the same skill now. If you’ve got one as a class skill, you have both. PF consolidated stuff like Spot & Listen and Balance & Tumble. That they missed this one was an error even Jacobs could see in his Q&A thread ages ago. We already essentially play this way, so let’s rip the bandaid off. The “new” skill is Knowledge (arcana), which is still a knowledge skill for all rules stuffs.

## Mind-Affecting Effects

Mind-affecting stuff is cool and opens up lots of creative problem solving that doesn’t necessarily reduce to “hit it until its hp go away”. They are also a big part of the arsenal of some cool classes. But 3.0 made immunity to them a defining trait of entire types of monsters and Paizo made that worse by heavily featuring one of them (undead) in almost every AP and focusing other APs on another type (constructs). In either situation, they’re common enough that it comes close to telling entire concepts to just go home or be content with Plan B for most of your career. That stinks. It’s so bad that Paizo ended up giving out ways to bypass the immunity in the form of archetypes, bloodlines, and metamagic up the wazoo.

Let’s cut the shit: Creatures that have intelligence scores have minds and are not immune to mind-affecting stuff by default. Some, which are particularly alien or insanity-themed may be immune (or dangerous to try stuff on) on a creature-to-creature basis. Targets that have no intelligence have no minds and consequently remain immune.

## Mindless Creatures and Alignment

A mindless creature is Neutral by default, because it’s incapable of making moral judgments. Mindless creatures powered by planar entities or touched by certain energies might be good, evil, lawful, or chaotic because of that but this is an exceptional case. Creating such creatures, even if they’re creepy Halloween monsters, is not an evil act and spells doing it do not have the [evil] tag. So your standard skeleton or zombie isn’t neutral evil; they’re just neutral.

Uncontrolled mindless creatures either act on pure instinct or according to instructions from their creators, which may have suffered some decay over the years. More elaborate instructions tend to decay faster, so “slay any who don’t bear my sign when they enter the room” is prone to decaying to “slay anything who enters the room”. This is basically how golems already work; I’m just expanding it.

## Monster Knowledge Checks

This is mostly codifying how we already do this, for completeness’ sake, with a few tweaks.

1. As a general rule, creatures below CR 10 use the 10+CR DCs. Creatures who answer to DC5+CR due to their great commonality are rarities. Goblins or orcs would, but they’re also so common and uncomplicated that you probably aren’t going to bother with a check. They kind of have to be decided on a case-by-case basis.
2. Creatures of CR 10 and up are, again as a general rule, rare enough to use 15+CR for their DC. Not that you’ll much notice by this point.
3. Actually unique or near unique creatures like demigods, demon lords, and company will answer to a 20+CR DC as a house rule. I don’t want to totally shut you out, but foes like this are either so obscure that little is known at all or are notorious enough that there’s an abundance of false information even among the learned and your check represents the things you’re confident are so. In general, dudes with boss mechanics are going to be at this tier.
4. If you hit the minimum DC to ID the creature, you also know its monster type and will have access to the general traits for that type. Monsters may not exhibit all of those traits, but there’ll be some cards in handouts that you can reference to help you out and possibly guide further questions. Because the information may not be perfectly accurate, you get the type traits as a freebie. This does not include any subtypes the target may have.
5. You may spend your questions on these general headings: special attacks, special defenses, weaknesses, best save, worst save, highest ability score, lowest ability score, and anything suitably similar that I didn’t think of. A general question will not give you hard numbers. In the event of a tie on best/worst style questions, I’ll roll a die in secret and tell you one based on the result.
6. If you want a hard number, you can instead ask a specific question. In that event, you get one number per question asked. So getting exact SR is one question, as is exact AC, save bonus, etc. Many of these things will show up quickly in a fight anyway, but if you need them right then you can have ‘em. You can ask a specific question even if you don’t know if the foe has the thing you’re asking about. You just risk wasting the question if you do.

## Noticing Spellcasting

Late-ish in PF1’s life, Paizo published a FAQ that told us what they said we all knew all along: when you do anything magical it is obvious because there are instant runes, fireworks, blaring trumpets, whatever. This is hot bullshit and everyone knew it. The most justification its defenders could come up with in the flavor is that Paizo often has art where casters are doing something that has FX...instead of just kind of standing there in a weird pose. This is dumb and the ways Paizo introduced to cast spells stealthily are shockingly bad. That badly screws with both lots of monsters clearly designed without such expectations and renders classes who have sidelines in sneakily doing magic in social situations basically unable to do that. So here’s the deal, overwritten a bit to reduce ambiguities:

1. If a spell’s effect is obvious, it remains obvious. There’s no hiding a fireball, a conjured 	creature, or a translucent spell construct. If the spell describes itself as doing or creating something visible, that’s what happens. Disintegrate shoots a ray. Burning hands comes out of your hands. But charm person just makes someone take a liking to you. It doesn’t do something like make you get spirals in your eyes or zap the target with a mind bolt.
2. Verbal and Somatic components are by default highly noticeable. DC 0 to spot. If you use metamagic feats to remove those components, they can't be noticed. You cannot conceal V or S components by mumbling or gesturing in your pockets, but if you cast in a dark room, while invisible, or with a lot of background noise they will become accordingly harder to spot. If you’re not casting with any visible components then you just need to stand fairly still and concentrate for 3-6 seconds, which usually isn’t the kind of thing that will set you out from the crowd.
3. Thought and Emotion components, mostly used by psychic magic, are not noticeable by default.
4. Spell-Like Abilities have no components and are only noticeable if their effects are.
5. You can still identify spells being cast and SLAs being used with Spellcraft, but only if you are aware they’re being done.
6. NPCs who don’t have Spellcraft may still notice that either they feel very differently or someone else is acting strange and make correlations based on where the target was and who was also there, but this is not automatic.
7. If you succeed a save against a non-obvious effect, you are aware that *something* just happened to you and that’s suitable cause for skill checks. If you fail and there’s no immediate, obvious, and out of the ordinary effect then you probably don’t know. So NPCs don’t necessarily know that you charmed them, but hold person causes paralysis and they will know that’s not normal for them.

## Old Edition Openish Door

Pathfinder1 is based on 3.5, which is based on 3e. The third editions are somewhat closer to each other than PF, but for the most part all three talk to and in the same rules language. There are some subtly different design sensibilities involved, which are a genuine mixed bag. Since you’ve all heard my gripes before, a couple of things Paizo did right: consolidating the skill list is a big one and the idea of simplifying combat maneuvers to a single mechanic is good, though the implementation made specializing in them an often poor idea in long-running games.

Since this stuff is close enough, and some of the 3e stuff is really cool, I am open to direct ports and/or Pathfinderization of 3e content if you’re interested in something. This is simplest with things like skills, spells, and magic items. In principle it also includes base and prestige classes and rules subsystems, but those obviously take a bit more doing. I can’t give you an answer on every build and every option because you only think Paizo churned out a lot of content. For most of 3e’s run, WotC was putting out at least one hardcover *per month*. There are some stinkers in there, of course, and some stuff that needs a nerf or might be too involved in old mechanics to translate, but here’s a general framework I’ll use if you’re asking for something:

1. If we have things named the same or similarly, the 3e name will probably need to change to minimize confusion.
2. If there is a clear PF equivalent, which is roughly as useful as the 3e stuff, we’ll probably stay with that unless there’s a good reason not to.
3. Skill ranks in 3e worked differently than in PF. If you want something that has a 3e skill rank requirement, subtract three from it to get the PF equivalent.
4. Herolab implementation and R20 stuff are going to be pretty much on your own. There might be things I can help with, but I have zero idea how to put prestige classes or base classes into the thing. I do have a pretty good idea of how to put a custom PC race in -it’s mostly just like making a custom monster- but that’s the present limit of my knowledge. If you want to do something ambitious like a full 3e class, 100% make a backup of the PC in your character vault on R20.
5. I have a fairly permissive attitude toward PC power -and I’m going to bring back a little of the monster power curve at least now and then- but there’s some stuff that’s still going to be too good. I still have Power Word No and also don’t want things to turn into an arms race between people willing to dive through books from the Cheney Regency and people who have lives. If something does turn out to be way better than expected and makes everyone else sad, we would need to revise.
6. I will probably, at least from time to time, slip in some 3e magic items and spells too. This isn’t really a house rule, but I feel like fair’s fair here even beyond just wanting to give you cool new-to-you shit.

## Precision Damage and Splash Weapons

You used to be able to have flask rogues, who would throw flasks of acid or alchemist’s fire or holy water or whatever for sneak attack damage, attacking touch. It was good, but usually a one round trick until you had greater invisibility on tap. It’s a ranged attack so you’d also have to be within 30 feet, etc.

Back in the PF alpha days, Jason Bulmahn denied this was a legal build. A dude showed him the rules text. The next version of the PF alpha included a sentence prohibiting you from adding precision damage to splash weapons. Just them. This is dumb and petty. Fuck that, you can do it again. If your attack is a legal sneak attack, you can use it with splash weapons. On a hit, your target takes full damage. Inflicting the splash damage does not get you a sneak attack or other precision damage because by definition you missed the sensitive spot. You can draw alchemical items like normal weapons.

## Quickdraw All Your Shit

Quickdraw lets you draw an item as a free action, including alchemical items, wands, across, and other things you could reasonably have stowed on a belt, up your sleeve, or in similar places. If you want to burn a feat to move, draw a scroll, and UMD it or read the thing fair and square, go nuts. It’s technically not the rule but we basically already play this way. Ain’t nobody wants to do inventory tetris and account for which butt cheek you peel back to get the universal solvent. You can also move and draw any item as part of a move action once you’re BAB +1. *You do still need to take the quickdraw feat to get quickdraw functionality.*

## Save-Or-Die Spells

SODs are often not fun for players or GMs. Ok, more often not fun for players. We GMs don’t get so attached to monsters we know exist to die anyway. However, turning them into damage spells that just do 10xCasterLevel or 2d6xCasterLevel makes them a bit meh and samey. The main reason to use one instead of an evocation there is either precision or you want to target a Fort save for some reason, since most of them are Fort saves. Many are also touch range. That’s kind of rough. The design goals here are to make SODs useful and tactically interesting, plus a touch more scary, without also making them always be “roll to keep playing”.

1. SOD spells reduce the target to -10 hp, -1hp/spell level. So a 5th level SOD puts you at -15 hp. A ninth level one puts you at -19. That probably kills most PCs, but does leave you in range for a breath of life to get you back up within the round.
2. This rule does not apply to SOD spells that take multiple rounds to kill you, like Suffocation, or which require multiple saves, like phantasmal killer, or which have a HD or hp cap on valid targets.  It also does not apply to Harm, which retains its static damage because it’s the reverse version of Heal and can’t reduce you to negative hp anyway.
3. Breath of Life and Raise dead work on spells with the [death] descriptor.

## Spell-Like Ability Action Economy

This is mostly relevant for monsters, but occasionally also for Team PC. There are two sets of rules for how SLAs work. One is, essentially, that they work exactly as if the spell is being cast except they use no components, unless otherwise stated, etc. You know the drill. The other is buried in the Universal Monster Rules, where it also tells you that an SLA is a standard action unless the monster entry tells you otherwise. This is a bit obscure and ambiguous and we’ve previously treated SLAs as inheriting the casting time of their spells. I think a lot of people do that automatically because it’s simpler and who reads the full UMR section?

Over my time running monsters, it’s become increasingly clear to me that SLAs probably should default to standard actions, even for spells that take a full round to cast. So I’m going to go with that from now on. The most likely times it will be relevant is when a monster has a summon buddies power. More seldom you might see something like dominate person thrown your way.

## Stat-Buffing Spells

We all love a good bull’s strength, bear’s endurance, fox’s cunning, etc. Having a buff like them is part of the assumed math of the game, even though it doesn’t tell you so. That means the items that do them are the ones you make a beeline for, which some people dislike. It also means you pay a big premium if you want more than one of your physical or mental stats buffed. You want it in an item because the durations are prohibitively short. This is hard on MAD classes and especially rough on martials. Thus:

1. The [animal] [attribute] spells that grant you an enhancement bonus to an ability score now have a duration of 1 hour per level. They come online with a +2 enhancement bonus (down from +4 in PF) but that bonus increases two points for every four levels thereafter. Thus you get +2 at 3rd, +4 at 7th, and +6 at 11th.
2. If you want more, or want to get ahead of the curve, or are worried about dispel magic, you can invest in items. Also see item slot changes above.

## Racial Mods and You

I’ve been pretty permissive with stuff from 3.5 and PF that lets you move around and change your racial mods. I want to continue doing that because, well, the game has enough race science stuff in it without doubling down. But there is a bit of weirdness in how 3.5 has subraces, PF has alt racial mods, and 3.5 also has some templates that are kind of like subraces and kind of like alt racial mods, but mechanically distinct. It’s hard to write a rule that would reconcile all of those and also be easy to parse, so let’s not do that.

Instead here’s what you can do: You can take any combination of these, but when you get done the sum of your racial ability modifiers can be no higher than +2 and no individual score can have a mod of higher than +4.

What might develop during the game remains its own thing. If the AP gives out +X to an ability score, you pick up an item, get a spell cast, or something, you become a lich werewolf vampire, etc, those aren’t capped or otherwise limited by this.

## And Now…the Chargen!

1. 25 point buy
2. Sathara is reasonably broad-minded about typical PC races. Unusual looking ones, or those who are or look a bit orcish, and especially the latter, may face some prejudice.
3. This region of the Realms is largely isolated from those which have limited access to firearms. As such, they’re not currently available in-game.
4. Psychic magic is a thing, but they call it “psionics.” Mechanically it’s still psychic magic. If you really want 3e psionics, we can talk about the third party PF adaptation of them.
5. Background skills are in use.
6. Additionally, if you have fewer than 4+int skills for your class *and you are not int-based*, you get bumped up to 4+int. If your casting or other main class feature is powered by intelligence, you are int-based.
7. You can select one knowledge skill and add it to your class skills at character creation. If you have all the knowledges already, you get Scholar as a bonus feat.
8. If you have full BAB, you gain [Combat Stamina](https://aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?Name=Combat%20Stamina&Category=Stamina%20and%20Combat%20Tricks) as a bonus feat at first level and anyone else can burn a feat on access. [The Stamina system](https://aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?Name=Stamina%20and%20Combat%20Tricks&Category=Pathfinder%20Unchained) is yours to play with.
9. Hit points: take max hp
10. Languages: The dominant tongue of Tashalan, which you get for free. Other locally prominent languages include Orcish, Gnome, Dwarven, Elven, and Abyssal.
11. Good favored enemy choices include: animal, humanoid (human, goblinoid, or orc), and outsider (evil).
12. You may take THREE traits, but one must be one of the campaign traits below. Since each of these comes with a drawback included, consider it a freebie. If you’d like to pick two, or more, traits from the same category then go ahead. If you want a campaign trait from a different AP, get in touch and we can see about a re-flavoring.
13. The game will begin in Sathara, on the festival of Highharvestide in the Year of the Bow.

## Campaign Traits

## Demonscarred

Somehow, the taint of the lower planes has entered your blood. You may not be a tiefling but the taint has unusual manifestations all the same.

	**Benefit: **Regardless of your actual alignment, spells and spell-like abilities with the evil descriptor treat you as if your alignment were evil. Magic items are similarly fooled.

	**Drawback:** Regardless of your actual alignment, spells and spell-like abilities with the good descriptor treat you as if your alignment were evil. Magic items are similarly fooled.

## Dream Haunted

You suffer strange visions of tortured landscapes and deformed monsters when you dream. In some, *you* are the deformed monster!

**Benefit: **You are used to fatigue and suffer no penalties when you become fatigued. When you become exhausted, you are treated instead as if you are fatigued.

	**Drawback:** You suffer a -2 penalty on saving throws against effects that cause madness or insanity, and on saving throws against sleep effects. If you are normally immune to sleep effects, you lose that immunity.

## Nobility

You are a scion of one of Cauldron’s noble families.

**Benefit: **You start play with an additional 200gp and gain a +1 bonus on all Diplomacy and Intimidate checks made against citizens of Cauldron or the nearby villages. Certain NPCs in the campaign may react more favorably to your presence.

	**Drawback:** You are well-known and recognizable, and suffer a -4 penalty on Disguise rolls made against citizens of Cauldron or the nearby villages. Certain NPCs in the campaign may react more poorly to your presence.

## Plague Haunted

One of your ancestors lived through a harrowing epidemic and you’ve inherited their fortitude...but also an ancestral fear of disease.

	**Benefit: **You gain a +2 bonus on all saving throws made to resist the effects of disease.

	**Drawback: **You suffer a -2 penalty on saving throws against fear while in places associated with disease (sickhouses, certain ruins, etc) or when fighting creatures that are diseased or can inflict disease through their spells or abilities.

## Scarred Soul

You’ve led a particularly tough life. Perhaps you’re an orphan, or maybe you suffered some sort of traumatic experience as a child. Whatever the cause, your childhood experiences have left you jumpy and haunted.

	**Benefit: **You gain a +2 bonus on initiative checks.

	**Drawback: **Your experiences have left your mind less able to deal with trauma, and as a result you suffer a -1 penalty on Will saves.

## Scion of Omnur

You are a descendant of the man who discovered Cauldron, helped settle the region, and aided in the defeat of an ancient demonic army.

	**Benefit: **Pride in your lineage girds your mind and soul. You gain a +2 morale bonus on saving throws against fear, death effects, and insanity or confusion.

	**Drawback: **Demons that encounter you in this region can instinctively sense your lineage and connection to their old enemy, and gain a +1 morale bonus on attack rolls and damage rolls when fighting you.

## Touched in the Head

You’re a little crazy.

**Benefit: **Your mind is disorganized and chaotic. You gain a +1 bonus on all saving throws against mind-affecting effects, save for those that cause confusion or insanity.

	**Drawback: **Your inability to concentrate for long makes you suffer a -1 penalty on all Wisdom-based skill checks.

## Wyrm Blooded

You are somehow descended from a black dragon. You may have some sort of distinctively draconic feature, be it reptilian eyes, scales on the back of your hands.

**Benefit: **You gain a +4 bonus on all saving throws against acid effects, a +2 bonus on swim checks, and a +1 bonus on perception checks.

	**Drawback: **Your body isn’t quite as limber as it should be. You take a -1 penalty on Reflex saves.
